Dzeroth Privacy Policy

Last updated: 1 November 2026

Dzeroth ("Dzeroth", "we", "us") is a text-first social app at dzeroth.com. This page explains what information we collect when you use it, why, where it's stored and how you can control it. We've tried to write it in plain language rather than legal boilerplate — if anything is unclear, email us.

We switch features on gradually, so some of the features described below may not be available on your account yet.

Who we are

Dzeroth is an early-stage, independently-run project built by a single founder based in India. There is no separate data protection team yet — privacy requests go straight to the founder at privacy@dzeroth.com.

Data we collect

  • Account data. When you sign up, Firebase Authentication (our identity provider) stores your email address and which sign-in method you used (email and password, Google or Apple), plus when you created the account and last signed in. If you sign in with Google or Apple, we receive your name and email address from them. Email and password accounts must verify their email address before creating a profile. We never see your password in readable form.
  • Profile data. Your handle, display name and bio, once you create a profile, and anything else you add to it: a profile photo, a header image, a location and a website (as you type them; the app doesn't use your device's location) and a post you pin. We also store whether your account is protected, when you joined, counts of your posts, followers and following, and your settings, such as the lists you pinned as tabs on your Home screen. Your profile is visible to other signed-in users, also when your account is protected.
  • Social graph. The accounts you follow and the accounts that follow you, the accounts you block, and the accounts you mute. For each follow we store when it happened. We also keep a follower count and a following count on each profile. Your follower and following counts are visible to other signed-in users. Your follower and following lists are too, unless your account is protected (see "Protected accounts" below). Your blocked and muted lists are not: only you can see them. Blocks and mutes are stored as lists of account IDs without a separate time for each entry. When you block someone, we also record on their side of the graph that you blocked them, so the block can be enforced. That record is never shown to them and never included in an export. If you ask to follow a protected account, or someone asks to follow yours, we store the request (who asked whom, and when) until it is accepted, declined or withdrawn. We also keep small per-day counters of how many follows, blocks and mutes you made, and of other actions such as posts, likes, uploads, reports and new message conversations, to enforce daily limits that prevent abuse.
  • Posts and activity. The posts you write, including replies, quotes and reposts: their text, when you posted them, the hashtags and @mentions in them, who can reply, and any labels you add (paid partnership, made with AI). Posts you schedule are stored until they are published or you cancel them. We also store which posts you like and which you bookmark.
  • Photos and GIFs. Images you add to posts, and your profile photo and header image, with each image's size, type and upload time, and any image description (alt text) you write. The app resizes and re-encodes photos on your device before uploading them, which removes details embedded in the file, such as the camera model and the location where the photo was taken. GIFs are not re-encoded, so that their animation is kept, but the app removes the comments, embedded text and other metadata blocks a GIF file can carry before uploading it, and our servers refuse a GIF that still contains them. See "Photos and GIFs" below.
  • Direct messages. The messages you send and receive, who they are between and when they were sent, whether a conversation is a message request, and when you last read each conversation. See "Direct messages" below.
  • Notifications. A list of notifications about activity that involves you (for example new followers, likes, replies, mentions and reposts), each with the account that caused it. If you allow push notifications on Android, we store a push token for each of your devices (up to 5) so that we can send them.
  • Reports. If you report a post, an account, a list or a message, we store your report: what you reported, the reason and any note you add. See "Reports and moderation" below.
  • Searches. We don't store what you search for on our servers. See "Search and trends" below.
  • Poll votes and lists. If you vote in a poll, we store your choice with your account. If you make a list, we store its name, its description, whether it is private and which accounts are on it. The "Polls" and "Lists" sections below explain who can see this and how long we keep it.
  • On your device. Your browser or phone keeps your sign-in session and a local cache of what you've recently seen in the app (profiles, the accounts you follow, your timeline, notifications, conversations and messages), so the app loads faster. Drafts of posts you haven't sent are also kept only on your device (see "Drafts" below). We do not use advertising or analytics cookies. The cache and your recent searches are deleted from the device when you sign out.
  • Technical data. Our servers keep operational logs with one line per request: which action was called, a shortened one-way hash of your user ID (not the ID itself), timing, result and database usage counts. Our own code does not write your IP address to these logs. The hash is a pseudonym, not anonymous: anyone who already knows your user ID could compute it. Google Cloud, which hosts our infrastructure, also records standard request details (which can include IP address and browser type) in its own platform logs and network security systems.

Why we collect it

  • To create and secure your account, and verify it's really you.
  • To run the features of the app: profiles, following, blocking and muting, posts and timelines, likes, reposts and bookmarks, photos and GIFs, direct messages, notifications, search, polls and lists.
  • To keep the service safe — detecting spam, abuse and rate-limit violations, checking uploaded images for unsafe content and reviewing reports.
  • To debug problems and keep the app reliable.
  • To meet legal obligations, such as responding to valid legal requests.

We do not sell your data, and we do not use it for third-party advertising.

Following, blocking and muting

  • Follows are public. Other signed-in users can see who you follow and who follows you. People who aren't signed in can't. If your account is protected, only you and the followers you have approved can see these lists.
  • Blocking. If you block someone, they can no longer view your profile, follow you or send you messages, and any follow or pending follow request between the two of you is removed in both directions.
  • Blocks and mutes are not disclosed. We don't tell someone that you blocked or muted them, and the app doesn't show anyone a list of who blocked them. Muting has no effect on the other person; it only changes what you see.
  • Limits. We can't promise that a blocked person can never work out they've been blocked. For example, they will notice that any follow between you disappeared, and that your profile no longer loads for them.

Protected accounts

  • Who sees what. If you protect your account, your posts, your follower and following lists and your lists are shown only to you and the followers you have approved. Your profile (including your name, handle, photo and bio) stays visible to signed-in users, and your account can still be found by its @handle.
  • Follow requests. People who want to follow you send a request. They become a follower only if you accept it. If you switch your account back to public, every pending request is accepted and those people start following you.
  • Limits. Protecting your account doesn't take back what people have already seen or saved, and images in your posts are files that anyone with their web address can open (see "Photos and GIFs" below).

Posts, likes and bookmarks

  • Posts. Your posts, replies, quotes and reposts can be seen by other signed-in users, except people you have blocked or who have blocked you, and, if your account is protected, anyone you haven't approved. For each post you choose who can reply.
  • Likes. The number of likes on a post is visible to anyone who can see the post, and the person who wrote it can be told in their notifications that you liked it. The list of posts you have liked is visible only to you.
  • Reposts. A repost appears on your profile and in your followers' timelines, and the person who wrote the original post can be notified.
  • Bookmarks. Only you can see your bookmarks. The person who wrote a post isn't told that you bookmarked it.
  • Scheduled posts. A post you schedule is stored on our servers until it is published or you cancel it. After that we keep a short record of it, without its text, for 7 days.
  • Deleting a post. When you delete a post, we delete it and, in the background, its images, likes and reposts. Replies and quotes that other people wrote belong to them and stay; a quote then shows that your post is no longer available.

Photos and GIFs

  • Checking images. To keep unsafe images off Dzeroth, we send the images you upload to Google Cloud Vision's SafeSearch feature, which rates how likely an image is to contain adult, violent or other unsafe content. For a GIF, we send a few of its frames and its preview image. We use only that rating: we don't ask Cloud Vision to recognise faces or read text. An image that fails the check is not published.
  • Who can see images. Images are stored as files with hard-to-guess web addresses. The app shows an image only to people who can see the post or profile it belongs to, but anyone who has an image's web address can open it, even without signing in. This includes images in posts from protected accounts.
  • Deleting images. When you delete a post, its images are deleted. An upload that never completes is deleted within about 2 days. An image you added to a post but then didn't post may be kept until you delete your account. If you replace or remove your profile photo or header image, the old file is deleted later, at the latest when you delete your account. A browser or a network cache that already holds a copy of an image may keep showing it for a while after we delete it.

Direct messages

  • Not end-to-end encrypted. Direct messages are stored on our servers in Google Cloud's Mumbai region, protected by Google's standard encryption at rest, but they are not end-to-end encrypted. People with access to our database can technically read them; we do so only for safety or legal reasons. Messages can be disclosed when the law requires it, for example under a valid court order.
  • Who can message you. If you and another person follow each other, their messages go to your inbox. Anyone else can send you up to 3 messages as a message request, which you can accept or ignore. People you have blocked, and people who have blocked you, can't message you. Messages from people you have muted arrive without a notification.
  • No read receipts or typing indicators. We store when you last read a conversation, to show your unread count, but we never show it to the other person.
  • Notifications. A push notification for a message says only that you have a new message: it contains no sender and no text.
  • How long we keep messages. There is no time limit. A message's text is kept until the account that sent it is deleted, or until we remove the message after a report. You can't delete a single message. Deleting a conversation removes it from your inbox only; the other person keeps their copy.
  • If you delete your account. The text of every message you sent is erased. The other person sees "Message deleted" in its place and "Deleted account" instead of your name, and keeps the messages they sent you.
  • Your export. Your data export includes the messages you sent, in full, and the messages you received only as the time they arrived, not their text.

Notifications and push

  • Your notifications are kept for 90 days, then deleted automatically.
  • Push notifications are sent only on Android, through Firebase Cloud Messaging (Google), and only if you allow them. A push shows a short general text, such as "New follower", and carries internal IDs that the app uses to open the right screen. It never contains the text of a post or a message, or anyone's handle. When you sign out, the app asks our servers to remove that device's push token. There are no push notifications on the web or on iPhone yet.

Search and trends

  • Search. Search looks up accounts by their @handle and posts by hashtag. We do not store what you search for on our servers. Your recent searches stay on your device and are deleted when you sign out. On the web, the browser history may keep the search page address.
  • Being found. Every account, including protected accounts, can be found by @handle. A protected account shows only its public profile in search.
  • Trends. Trends are computed from public posts. Posts by protected accounts are never counted, and no personal data is stored with a trend.

Polls

  • Your vote is stored with your account. When you vote in a poll, we store your choice and when you voted. If you change your vote, we also store when you last changed it and how many times you have changed it. This lets us count one vote per account, show you your own vote and let you change it.
  • Individual choices are hidden from other users. No other user, including the person who made the poll, can see which choice you picked or a list of who voted. Anyone who can see the poll sees the total number of votes. While the poll is open, the number of votes for each choice is shown to the person who made it and to people who have voted (and to everyone if we pause voting on polls). Once it closes, everyone who can see the poll can see them.
  • Small polls can give a vote away. On a poll with only a few votes, someone watching the counts may be able to work out how a particular person voted, for example if they know that person has just voted. Changing your vote moves one choice's count down and another's up while the total stays the same, so it can show the poll's author and people who have already voted both your earlier and your new choice. People who haven't voted see only the total while the poll is open, and a change doesn't move it.
  • Changing your vote. You can change your vote while the poll is open, up to a limited number of times. Your earlier choice is overwritten, not kept as a history. Once the poll has closed, votes can't be cast or changed.
  • Blocking. If you block the person who made a poll, or they block you, you can't vote in their polls or change your vote. A vote you cast before the block is kept and still counts.
  • How long we keep your vote. Until 30 days after the poll closes. After that it is deleted, usually within a day (apart from the database backups described below). If you delete your account, your votes are deleted with it; the poll's totals still include your vote, but the record of how you voted is deleted.
  • Your export. Your data export lists the poll votes we still hold: which poll, your choice, when you voted and, if you changed it, when you last did, and how many times.
  • No polls about sensitive personal information. Polls must not ask people for sensitive personal information, such as health, religion or beliefs, political opinions, sex life or sexual orientation, ethnicity, union membership, or genetic or biometric data. See our community rules.

Drafts

  • Drafts stay on your device. A draft is a post you have started but not sent, saved by you or automatically while you write. It is stored only on the device where you wrote it: in the app's storage, or in your browser's storage if you use the website. A draft keeps the text, any poll choices, who can reply and any labels you chose; photos and GIFs are never saved in a draft. Drafts are not sent to us until you post or schedule them, so we can't see or recover them and they are not part of your data export.
  • Signing out. Drafts stay on the device when you sign out, so they are still there when you sign back in. Other accounts used on that device can't see them in the app, but someone with access to the device or browser could read them with technical tools. On a shared device, delete your drafts before you sign out. If your account has drafts on the device when you sign out, the app asks whether to keep them or delete them first. While it has drafts there, Settings also shows "Delete unsent posts on this device".
  • 30-day limit. A draft you haven't edited for 30 days is no longer shown, and it is deleted from that device the next time you open the app (or your drafts) there while signed in to that account.
  • Deleting your account. When you delete your account in the app, the account's drafts on that device are deleted at the same time. Drafts on your other devices are not deleted automatically, and neither are drafts on any device if your account is deleted some other way (for example after an email request). To remove them, delete them in Drafts on that device before you delete your account, or clear the app's data or the browser's site data there.

Lists

  • Your lists. If you make a list, we store its name, its description, whether it is private and which accounts are on it. A new list is private unless you choose to make it public. Only you can see a private list.
  • Public lists. Other signed-in users can see your public lists, including their names, descriptions and members, except people you have blocked. If your account is protected, your lists (public ones included) are visible only to you and the followers you have approved.
  • Being added to a list. People aren't told when they are added to a list. Someone can add you only if neither of you has blocked the other and, if your account is protected, only if they are a follower you have approved. If your account is protected, your posts show up in someone's list only for people you have approved.
  • Your export. Your data export includes the lists you made, with their name, description, privacy setting and the account IDs of their members. It also covers lists made by other people that include you. A public list is shown with its name and its owner's handle. Every other list that includes you is only counted, with no name, owner or other members: private lists, lists we have hidden for breaking our rules, lists of a protected account you don't follow, and lists whose owner's account is no longer active.

Reports and moderation

  • Reporting. You can report a post, an account, a list or a message. We never tell the person you reported who reported them.
  • What a report keeps. A report keeps a copy of what was reported as it was at the time (for example a post's text and its author's handle, or an account's profile details), so we can review it even if it is changed or deleted. Reports are kept until we have dealt with them and then for 90 days, after which they are deleted automatically.
  • Reported messages. If someone reports a message you sent them, we keep a copy of that one message (its text, your handle and when it was sent) so we can review it. The copy is kept for at most 180 days after the report, and no longer than 90 days after we resolve it, even if you delete your account in the meantime.
  • What we can do. If something breaks our community rules, we can remove a post or a message, hide a list, clear parts of a profile (such as a website or a photo), or suspend an account. When we remove a post, we keep a short record of it (the post's ID and its author's account ID) for 32 days, so that it disappears from everyone's timelines.
  • Reports you made are kept as safety evidence. If you delete your account, they are kept without your account ID. A note you added to a report we hadn't dealt with yet stays with that report until it is deleted, 90 days after we resolve it.

Who we share it with

We don't sell your data and we don't use it for advertising. We use these service providers, who process data on our behalf (a "processor" in privacy terminology):

  • Google Cloud and Firebase (Google LLC). Firebase Authentication (sign-in, including Google sign-in), Cloud Firestore (our database), Cloud Run (our servers), Cloud Storage (images and data export files), Pub/Sub and Cloud Scheduler (background tasks), Cloud Logging (server logs), Firebase Hosting (serves the website and passes app requests to our servers), Cloud Vision (checks uploaded images, see "Photos and GIFs"), Firebase Cloud Messaging (push notifications on Android) and Firebase App Check (in the Android app, it uses Google Play Integrity to confirm that requests come from our genuine app). We use Google as a processor. Under Google's Firebase Data Processing and Security Terms and Cloud Data Processing Addendum, Google processes customer data to provide, secure and monitor the services and as we instruct through our use of them. Please read those terms for the details; we don't restate them here.
  • Google, when you load the web app. The web app loads its rendering engine, fonts and the Firebase SDK scripts from Google's servers (gstatic.com), and Google sign-in loads Google scripts and a Google sign-in window. When that happens Google may see your IP address and standard request details such as your browser type. This is based on inspecting our published web app; we have not yet confirmed it with a browser network trace. Images are loaded straight from Google Cloud Storage, in the web app and in the mobile app, so Google sees the same request details when an image is shown.
  • Apple. Only if you choose Sign in with Apple. Apple handles that sign-in and passes us your name and email address (or an Apple relay address if you chose to hide your email).
  • ImprovMX and Gmail (email). Mail sent to privacy@dzeroth.com is forwarded by ImprovMX, an email forwarding service, to the founder's Gmail inbox (Google). Anything you write in a privacy request, including your email address and account details, passes through those two services. Only email us what is needed for the request.

We don't use analytics or a crash-reporting service, and the app shows no advertising. If we add analytics or crash reporting, we will describe it here before we switch it on. Web bot protection (Firebase App Check with reCAPTCHA) is not switched on; if it is, this section will say so.

Where your data is stored

Your profile, social graph, poll votes and lists are stored in Google Cloud's Mumbai (asia-south1) region, and our servers run there too. Drafts stay on your device. Sign-in records are held by Firebase Authentication, which does not let us choose a region. Google may process them in other countries. The rest of our database is in Mumbai too, including your posts, likes, bookmarks, direct messages, notifications and reports. Images you upload, and the data export files you request, are stored in Google Cloud's United States (us-central1, Iowa) region, because that's where Google Cloud's free storage tier is available. We use Cloud Vision's global service, and Firebase Cloud Messaging does not let us choose a region, so images being checked and push notifications may be processed in other countries. All of it is protected by Google's standard encryption at rest.

How long we keep it

Where a time is given below, the automatic deletion usually finishes within a day after it.

  • Account and profile data: for as long as your account exists.
  • Follows, blocks and mutes: for as long as your account exists, or until you unfollow, unblock or unmute. They are deleted when your account is deleted (see below).
  • Follow requests: until they are accepted, declined or withdrawn, or either account is deleted.
  • Posts, replies, quotes and reposts: until you delete them or your account.
  • Likes and bookmarks: until you undo them or delete your account.
  • Scheduled posts: until they are published or you cancel them; then a record without the post's text for 7 days.
  • Images: as long as the post or profile they belong to. See "Photos and GIFs" above for uploads you don't post and for replaced profile images.
  • Direct messages: no time limit. See "Direct messages" above.
  • Notifications: 90 days, then deleted automatically.
  • Push tokens: until you sign out on that device, the token stops working or you delete your account. We keep tokens for at most 5 devices per account.
  • Poll votes: until 30 days after the poll closes, then usually deleted within a day; sooner if you delete your account. See "Polls" above.
  • Lists you make: until you delete them or your account.
  • Drafts: only on your device. See "Drafts" above for when they are deleted.
  • Reports: until we have dealt with them, then 90 days; a copy of a reported message at most 180 days. See "Reports and moderation" above.
  • Duplicate-request records (see below): 24 hours, then deleted automatically, usually within a day.
  • Data export files: 7 days, then deleted.
  • Server logs: kept for about 30 days (Google Cloud Logging's default), then deleted.
  • Database backups: weekly, each kept for 14 days, then deleted.
  • Mail to privacy@dzeroth.com: kept in the founder's Gmail inbox. We have not set a fixed retention period for these emails yet.

Your rights

You can ask us to:

  • Access the personal data we hold about you.
  • Correct inaccurate data.
  • Delete your account and associated data.
  • Export your data in a portable format (JSON). An export contains your profile, your sign-in record (email, sign-in method, account creation and last sign-in times) and your social graph: the accounts you follow, your followers, the accounts you blocked and the accounts you muted (IDs and handles), and your pending follow requests. It also contains your posts (including replies, quotes, reposts and scheduled posts), a list of your images with their web addresses (not the image files), the posts you liked, reposted and bookmarked, your notifications, the devices registered for push (platform and dates, never the push token), the reports you made, and your direct messages as described in "Direct messages" above. It never includes who has blocked you, because that is information about other people and revealing it would defeat blocking. It also includes your poll votes, the lists you made and the lists made by other people that include you, as described in "Polls" and "Lists" above; drafts are not included, because they are kept only on your device.
  • Withdraw consent where we rely on it, or raise a grievance about how we've handled your data.

To do any of these, email privacy@dzeroth.com from the email address on your account, so we can confirm it is you. We handle email requests by hand and will get back to you within 30 days; an export is sent to the email address on your account.

If Download my data and Delete account appear in the app's Settings, you can also do those two things yourself. An export you request in the app is usually ready within minutes. Its download link works for 15 minutes (you can get a new link while the file exists), you can request one export a day, and the file is deleted after 7 days. Deleting your account in the app asks you to sign in again first if you haven't signed in recently.

When your account is deleted, we delete your profile, your handle (which frees it for others), your sign-in account, your posts (including replies, quotes, reposts and scheduled posts) and their images, your profile photo and header image, your likes and bookmarks, your notifications and push tokens, your follows and followers, your pending follow requests, your blocked and muted lists, any data exports you requested and your daily limit counters. You are also removed from other people's follower, following and block lists and from the notifications you caused in their lists, and the follower, following, like and repost counts on their profiles and posts are corrected. We also delete your poll votes and the lists you made, and remove you from lists made by other people. Drafts are kept on your devices, not by us (see "Drafts" above). Once deletion starts it can't be undone. Your sign-in account is disabled within seconds, and for a typical account the rest is done within minutes.

In your direct messages, the text of every message you sent is erased, as described in "Direct messages" above. Reports you made are kept without your account ID.

What can remain after deletion, and for how long. Where a time is given in the list below, the automatic deletion usually finishes within a day after it. These are the only records in our systems (including the services Google runs for us) that can still contain your account ID, your handle or something you wrote after your account is deleted:

  • Duplicate-request records, up to 24 hours. To avoid acting twice on the same request, our database keeps a short record of recent actions that contains your account ID (an internal identifier, not your name or handle). Each record is deleted automatically 24 hours after it was made.
  • Other people's private block and mute lists, until cleaned up. If someone blocked or muted you, your account ID may stay in that person's private list. Those lists are cleaned up the next time their owner views them, and we have not set a fixed date for a complete clean-up. Only the owner can have the list read back, the app does not show accounts that no longer exist, and the list is not part of any profile.
  • Mentions and replies in other people's posts. If someone else mentioned your handle in their post, or replied to one of your posts (a reply keeps the handle it replied to), that post belongs to them and stays as they wrote it.
  • Reports about you, until 90 days after they are resolved. If someone reported you or something you posted, the report, with its copy of what was reported and your handle at the time, is kept as safety evidence until 90 days after we resolve it, then deleted automatically.
  • A reported message, at most 180 days. If someone reported a message you sent, our copy of that one message is kept for at most 180 days after the report and no longer than 90 days after we resolve it.
  • A note on a report you made, until 90 days after it is resolved. If we hadn't dealt with a report you made when your account was deleted, the note you wrote stays with that report (without your account ID) until it is deleted, 90 days after we resolve it.
  • Identifiers derived from your account ID. Some internal record IDs, for example those of a conversation you were in or of a report you made, are one-way hashes that include your account ID. They can't be turned back into your account ID, but someone who already knows it could check it against them. They last as long as those records: a conversation until the other person's account is deleted too, a report until 90 days after we resolve it.
  • A notification created at the moment of deletion, up to 90 days. In rare cases, a notification about something you did (for example a like) can be added to someone's list at the very moment your deletion runs. It then stays until it expires, 90 days after it was made.
  • Database backups, up to 14 days. Backups are taken weekly and each is deleted after 14 days. We don't restore deleted accounts from them.
  • Internal background task messages, up to 7 days. The deletion, like other background work such as notifications, runs as background tasks. The messages that drive them can contain your account ID and are kept by Google Cloud for at most 1 day, or at most 7 days if a message has to be set aside after repeated failures.
  • Device-level diagnostics. The app has no crash reporting or analytics today. If we add them, that data will be tied to your device rather than to your account, so it can't be found or deleted by account; we will describe it on this page before we switch it on.
  • Server logs, about 30 days. Our logs contain only a hashed version of your user ID, never the ID itself or your email, and expire after about 30 days.
  • Email you sent us, with no fixed limit yet. Mail you sent to privacy@dzeroth.com stays in the founder's Gmail inbox after your account is deleted, with your email address and what you wrote (see "Who we share it with" and "How long we keep it" above).

Not in that list, for the reason given with each:

  • Drafts on your devices. Drafts are stored only on your devices, and deleting your account in the app deletes them only on that device (see "Drafts" above). We never hold them.
  • Poll totals. Your vote stays counted in the totals of the polls you voted in, but once your votes are deleted nothing we keep links it to you (apart from the database backups listed above).
  • The other person's side of a conversation. The messages other people sent you stay in their copy of the conversation (see "Direct messages" above). They are theirs and contain nothing you wrote.
  • Copies outside our systems. Anything someone else saved, copied or took a screenshot of, and images still held in a browser or network cache (see "Photos and GIFs" above). We don't hold these copies.

Age requirement

Dzeroth is intended for people aged 18 and over. The app does not currently check your age at sign-up. If you believe someone under 18 has created an account, email us at privacy@dzeroth.com.

Security

We rely on Firebase Authentication for sign-in and Google Cloud's infrastructure security, encryption at rest and access controls. Every request to our servers needs a valid sign-in, and the database can't be read directly from the app or browser. This means people who aren't signed in can't see profiles or follower lists. The exception is images: anyone who has an image's web address can open it (see "Photos and GIFs" above). Direct messages are not end-to-end encrypted (see "Direct messages" above).
