dZeroth Privacy Policy

Last updated: 30 September 2026

dZeroth ("dZeroth", "we", "us") is a text-first social app at dzeroth.com. This page explains what information we collect when you use it, why, where it's stored and how you can control it. We've tried to write it in plain language rather than legal boilerplate — if anything is unclear, email us.

Who we are

dZeroth is an early-stage, independently-run project built by a single founder based in India. There is no separate data protection team yet — privacy requests go straight to the founder at privacy@dzeroth.com.

Data we collect

  • Account data. When you sign up, Firebase Authentication (our identity provider) stores your email address and which sign-in method you used (email and password, Google or Apple), plus when you created the account and last signed in. If you sign in with Google or Apple, we receive your name and email address from them. Email and password accounts must verify their email address before creating a profile. We never see your password in readable form.
  • Profile data. Your handle, display name and bio, once you create a profile. Your profile is visible to other signed-in users. Avatar images are planned but not available yet.
  • Social graph. The accounts you follow and the accounts that follow you, the accounts you block, and the accounts you mute. For each follow we store when it happened. We also keep a follower count and a following count on each profile. Your follower and following counts, and your follower and following lists, are visible to other signed-in users. Your blocked and muted lists are not: only you can see them. Blocks and mutes are stored as lists of account IDs without a separate time for each entry. When you block someone, we also record on their side of the graph that you blocked them, so the block can be enforced. That record is never shown to them and never included in an export. We also keep small per-day counters of how many follows, blocks and mutes you made, to enforce daily limits that prevent abuse.
  • Posts and activity. Posts, image uploads, likes and notifications are planned and don't exist yet. When they ship, we'll store what you write, upload and like, and update this policy first.
  • On your device. Your browser or phone keeps your sign-in session and a local cache of your profile and social graph, so the app loads faster. We do not use advertising or analytics cookies.
  • Technical data. Our servers keep operational logs with one line per request: which action was called, a shortened one-way hash of your user ID (not the ID itself), timing, result and database usage counts. Our own code does not write your IP address to these logs. The hash is a pseudonym, not anonymous: anyone who already knows your user ID could compute it. Google Cloud, which hosts our infrastructure, also records standard request details (which can include IP address and browser type) in its own platform logs and network security systems.

Why we collect it

  • To create and secure your account, and verify it's really you.
  • To run the core features of the app: profiles, following, blocking and muting, and (as they ship) posts and a timeline.
  • To keep the service safe — detecting spam, abuse and rate-limit violations.
  • To debug problems and keep the app reliable.
  • To meet legal obligations, such as responding to valid legal requests.

We do not sell your data, and we do not use it for third-party advertising.

Following, blocking and muting

  • Follows are public. Other signed-in users can see who you follow and who follows you. People who aren't signed in can't.
  • Blocking. If you block someone, they can no longer view your profile or follow you, and any follow between the two of you is removed in both directions.
  • Blocks and mutes are not disclosed. We don't tell someone that you blocked or muted them, and the app doesn't show anyone a list of who blocked them. Muting has no effect on the other person; it only changes what you see.
  • Limits. We can't promise that a blocked person can never work out they've been blocked. For example, they will notice that any follow between you disappeared, and that your profile no longer loads for them.

Who we share it with

We don't sell your data and we don't use it for advertising. We use these service providers, who process data on our behalf (a "processor" in privacy terminology):

  • Google Cloud and Firebase (Google LLC). Firebase Authentication (sign-in, including Google sign-in), Cloud Firestore (our database), Cloud Run (our servers), Cloud Logging (server logs) and Firebase Hosting (serves the website and passes app requests to our servers). We use Google as a processor. Under Google's Firebase Data Processing and Security Terms and Cloud Data Processing Addendum, Google processes customer data to provide, secure and monitor the services and as we instruct through our use of them. Please read those terms for the details; we don't restate them here.
  • Google, when you load the web app. The web app loads its rendering engine, fonts and the Firebase SDK scripts from Google's servers (gstatic.com), and Google sign-in loads Google scripts and a Google sign-in window. When that happens Google may see your IP address and standard request details such as your browser type. This is based on inspecting our published web app; we have not yet confirmed it with a browser network trace.
  • Apple. Only if you choose Sign in with Apple. Apple handles that sign-in and passes us your name and email address (or an Apple relay address if you chose to hide your email).
  • ImprovMX and Gmail (email). Mail sent to privacy@dzeroth.com is forwarded by ImprovMX, an email forwarding service, to the founder's Gmail inbox (Google). Anything you write in a privacy request, including your email address and account details, passes through those two services. Only email us what is needed for the request.

Not in use yet, and will be added here before they are: image storage and automated image moderation with Google Cloud Vision (uploaded images will be sent to Google to be checked), push notifications, crash reporting and analytics. Today the app has no analytics, no crash-reporting service, no push notifications and no image uploads. Web sign-in bot protection (Firebase App Check with reCAPTCHA) is also not switched on; if it is, this section will say so.

Where your data is stored

Your profile and social graph are stored in Google Cloud's Mumbai (asia-south1) region, and our servers run there too. Sign-in records are held by Firebase Authentication, which does not let us choose a region. Google may process them in other countries. When image uploads ship, images will be stored in the United States, because that's where Google Cloud's free storage tier is available. All of it is protected by Google's standard encryption at rest.

How long we keep it

  • Account and profile data: for as long as your account exists.
  • Follows, blocks and mutes: for as long as your account exists, or until you unfollow, unblock or unmute. They are deleted when your account is deleted (see below).
  • Server logs: kept for about 30 days (Google Cloud Logging's default), then deleted.
  • Database backups: weekly, each kept for 14 days, then deleted.
  • Mail to privacy@dzeroth.com: kept in the founder's Gmail inbox. We have not set a fixed retention period for these emails yet.

Your rights

You can ask us to:

  • Access the personal data we hold about you.
  • Correct inaccurate data.
  • Delete your account and associated data.
  • Export your data in a portable format (JSON). Today an export contains your profile, your sign-in record (email, sign-in method, account creation and last sign-in times) and your social graph: the accounts you follow, your followers, the accounts you blocked and the accounts you muted (IDs and handles). It never includes who has blocked you, because that is information about other people and revealing it would defeat blocking. We prepare it by hand with an internal tool (there is no in-app export yet) and send it to the email address on the account.
  • Withdraw consent where we rely on it, or raise a grievance about how we've handled your data.

To do any of these, email privacy@dzeroth.com from the email address on your account, so we can confirm it is you. Until in-app deletion and export tools ship, we handle these requests manually and will get back to you within 30 days.

When your account is deleted, we delete your profile, your handle (which frees it for others), your sign-in account, your follows and followers, your blocked and muted lists and your daily limit counters. You are also removed from other people's follower, following and block lists, and their counts are corrected. Deleted data can remain in our database backups for up to 14 days, as described above. Server logs containing the hashed version of your user ID stay until they expire (about 30 days). One thing can outlast that: if someone muted you, your account ID (an internal identifier, not your name or handle) may remain in that person's private muted list until a clean-up that removes it ships. We have not set a date for it. Only the person who muted you can have that list read back, the app does not show it for accounts that no longer exist, and it is not part of any profile.

Age requirement

dZeroth is intended for people aged 18 and over. The app does not currently check your age at sign-up. If you believe someone under 18 has created an account, email us at privacy@dzeroth.com.

Security

We rely on Firebase Authentication for sign-in and Google Cloud's infrastructure security, encryption at rest and access controls. Every request to our servers needs a valid sign-in, and the database can't be read directly from the app or browser. This means people who aren't signed in can't see profiles or follower lists. No system is perfectly secure, but we keep access to production data restricted and monitor for unusual activity.

Changes to this policy

We may update this policy as dZeroth adds features (posts, media, likes, notifications and beyond). We'll update the "Last updated" date above when we do. If a change is significant, we'll try to let you know in-app.
